> "Austerity without awareness is just the ego in fasting clothes."

## From Form to the Witness

Spiritual life is complicated, and it rarely follows a neat or logical blueprint. For someone guided more by gut and feeling than by rigid intellectual arguments, the journey unfolds slowly through layers that have to be felt rather than debated.

It often begins with intense devotion to form and symbols—where faith is physical, tangible, and deeply comforting. Over time, that reverence tends to soften and expand into Nature, feeling the divine in its vastness, its complexity, and the quiet rhythm of existence. Eventually, the inquiry turns inward to Advaita and the Self, seeing that the awareness witnessing reality is the very same light reflected across everyone and everything.

Between each of these shifts, there are long, quiet pauses. You take time to absorb, digest, and let the previous beliefs settle into your bones. You step away from noise, practice internal restraint, and live through quiet periods of contemplation. 

This inner discipline feels like austerity—a quiet fire meant to burn away distraction and pride.

Yet right at this point, a silent parasite takes root.

---

## The Holy Mask

Austerity is meant to dissolve the ego, but the ego has a remarkable way of surviving. 

When someone chases material wealth, status, or praise, their ego is obvious and easy to spot. But when you spend years in contemplation, self-restraint, and quiet study, the ego doesn't simply disappear. It upgrades its wardrobe.

It puts on the robes of spirituality and begins whispering subtle narratives:

*That you have endured while others remained distracted.*  
*That you digested the deeper layers while others only skimmed the surface.*  
*That your quietness, your intuition, and your discipline make you somewhat more evolved than ordinary people.*

Instead of the fire burning the ego, the ego quietly hijacks the fire. It warms its hands over the ashes of its own discipline and congratulates itself on being wise. 

This subtle superiority is a parasite. It slowly eats away at genuine wisdom from the inside, leaving only an image behind.

---

## The Uncomfortable Inquest

When you sit in stillness and look directly at this tendency, hard questions arise:

Did I absorb these truths out of a pure longing for reality, or did part of me want to impress someone?  
What am I trying to project when I speak about depth, intuition, or philosophy?  
Who is this unseen audience I feel the need to perform for?

Even when we believe we are sharing insights to illuminate or connect, a quiet current of performance often runs underneath. We want to be recognized as someone who *knows*, who feels deeply, who has touched something profound.

The moment we crave recognition for our understanding, wisdom turns into currency.

---

## The Paradox of Losing Awareness

The most humbling part of this realization is the recurring amnesia of the mind.

You can see through this trap with complete clarity. You can reflect on it, write about it, and understand the mechanics of the spiritual ego inside out. Yet, hours later in an ordinary conversation, the subtle comparison sneaks right back in. Someone says something you find shallow, and before you even realize it, a quiet condescension fires in the background.

Why do we lose awareness even after realizing this a gazillion times?

Intellectual knowing is not stillness. Repeating reminders about humility is still an activity of thought, and the ego loves discussing its own death because it keeps the ego at the center of attention. Conditioning runs deep; comparison is the default habit of the human mind, and the moment attention relaxes, the old program reboots.

Losing awareness is not a defeat; it is simply the movement of thought. The real trap is when we beat ourselves up for forgetting, creating yet another layer of self-importance through guilt.

---

## The Loop of Overthinking and Eternal Surrender

As you go deeper and deeper into this self-inquiry, it is easy to get caught in an endless loop of thoughts. 

The mind begins to analyze the mind analyzing the ego:  
*Am I being aware, or is this just another subtle trick? Am I overthinking my overthinking?*

The seeker looking for clarity gets bombarded by a storm of unanswered questions. You realize that the intellect cannot think its way out of thought; a knife cannot cut itself.

I am still figuring all this out, but sitting with this confusion makes me wonder: maybe this is why concepts like a **Guru**, **God**, or **total surrender** were introduced in ancient traditions. 

Not as rigid dogmas, but as a compassionate circuit breaker for an exhausted mind.

When the mental loops become unbearable, surrender gives you permission to put down the heavy backpack. You hand the keys over—to God, to the Guru, or to the mystery of existence itself. You admit that you cannot solve this with logic alone, and in that admission, the overthinking finally falls silent.

---

## Radical Ordinariness

If intellectual analysis and aggressive self-policing cannot dissolve this subtle parasite, what can?

The answer is quiet and simple.

When you catch yourself feeling superior or wanting to project wisdom, do not fight it. Fighting only gives it energy and turns it into a dramatic battle. Instead, notice it with a gentle, inward smile: *Ah, look at you trying to be special again.* The moment it is seen without defense, the illusion deflates.

Remember too that whatever depth, peace, or clarity you have experienced never belonged to your personal identity. It was simply the nature of reality, like rain falling on dry earth. You didn't invent the rain; you were merely fortunate enough to stand in it.

The ultimate aim of the inner path is not to become an extraordinary, luminous figure floating above the world. 

It is to become **completely, unapologetically ordinary**.

To do daily chores without needing a cosmic narrative. To listen to someone without an itch to correct them or display depth. To sit in a room, knowing what you know, and be entirely content being unremarkable, unacknowledged, and unseen.

---

## Final Reflection

When you no longer need anyone to know what you know, the burden of projection drops.

True wisdom doesn't wear a badge, and it doesn't need to win an argument. It rests quietly in the background—content just to be alive, aware, and empty of pretense.
